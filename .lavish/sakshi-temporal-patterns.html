Sakshi / temporal evidence intelligence / 02 October 2026

The sequence can matter
more than a single message.

Count distinct retained contacts. Preserve boundaries and source uncertainty. Describe recurrence, pressure and changes in wording with actual evidence, not automatic legal conclusions or predictions of violence.

“Harmless” text can still contribute to a pattern. Six ordinary-looking contact attempts after a reviewed stop request differ from six consensual logistics messages. Counts alone cannot establish unwantedness, identity, intent or complete conversation history.

Research report: research/temporal-harassment-patterns.md. Machine-readable design: data/sakshi-event-schema.json. All example timelines are synthetic. No Sakshi Android/model/battery benchmark was executed. Laya is deferred under the current project rules.

An event must carry its uncertainty

Two times, not one

Occurrence versus availability

Time interval, basis, precision and timezone; separate capture/analysis availability and review time. A historical import changes retrospective analysis, not when Sakshi originally detected it.

Actor association

A name is not identity

Case-scoped alias, app/profile/conversation hints, direction and association review. Two sources named “Alex” stay separate without a reviewed link.

Annotation-level review

Save does not accept every tag

Preservation confirmation, category acceptance, boundary scope and confidence semantics remain separate. Review priority is not a danger score.

JSON validation is not evidence validation

The schema checks types/enums, required source refs, confidence semantics and candidate/vault conditions. Application checks must enforce reference existence, matching hashes, valid offsets/time bounds, case isolation, canonical-link cycles, knowledge cutoffs, consent and logical expiry.

Text anchors use half-open Unicode code-point offsets. Kotlin/Java String uses UTF-16 indices; test conversion. A hash supports integrity checking, not authenticity or legal admissibility.

Events → timeline → recurrence → explanation

Privacy limits coverage. Default selective notification retention can miss harmless-looking buildup. Use user-selected conversation evidence for the hackathon. RAM counters cannot reconstruct discarded text; persistent metadata history is a separate disclosed opt-in, not an automatic expansion.

    B / wording transition

    Insult → explicit harm statement

    1. 1 Oct 18:00B1: “You're useless.” Reviewed verbal abuse.
    2. 18:20B2: “You'll regret refusing.” Consequence/intent uncertain.
    3. 2 Oct 08:00B3: “I will hurt you tonight.” Reviewed explicit harm wording.

    C / benign frequency contrast

    Density rose. Harassment is not established.

    3 → 12

    Three retained incoming messages in 08:00-09:00, twelve in 09:00-10:00. Same selected export coverage, consensual deadline logistics and two-way conversation.

    Explain: observed density is 4x in comparable selected hours. No unwanted-contact conclusion follows from this arithmetic.

    D / recurrence + missingness

    A gap is not a period without contact

    1. 1 Oct 10:00D0: private disengagement note; stop-request communication unknown.
    2. 10:10-10:20D1-D3: three retained contacts.
    3. 11:00-09:0022-hour collector outage across dates; unknown coverage.
    4. 2 Oct 09:10D4-D5: two retained contacts at 09:10 / 09:15.

    Explain: five retained observations in two observed episodes after the marker. No claim of silence, no replies, or a communicated-boundary violation.

    E / repeated location pressure

    Request, refusal, proof demand, consequence

    1. 20:00E1: “Send your live location.”
    2. 20:05E2: user refuses location sharing.
    3. 20:07E3: demand for a photo proving whereabouts.
    4. 20:10E4: stated consequence involving the user's manager.

    Explain: repeated location/proof requests and a consequence after refusal. “Possible controlling pressure” is an interpretation, not legal coercive control or proof of actual monitoring.

    F / five callbacks, three messages, two people called Alex

    Payloads: [F1], [F1,F2], [F1,F2], [F1,F2,F3], [F1,F2,F3], with distinguishable fixture source ids. Expected count: three contacts, not five callbacks or eleven cumulative entries. If two distinct source records both say “Hi,” keep both.

    Two different app/profile scopes named “Alex” stay separate without a reviewed association. Text/name similarity is not cross-app identity proof.

    Arithmetic resource examples

    10,000 × 256-byte compact records = 2.56 MB; 10×10 float32 transitions = 400 bytes. These exclude text, JSON/database/index/encryption overhead, runtime and originals.

    10,000 × 384-D float32 embeddings = 15.36 MB before index/model memory. Sensitive embeddings require retention controls.

    LLM: optional explanation, not counting

    Pass computed counts/time bounds, selected anchored excerpts, reviewed boundary scope and limitations. Bound prompt/output, verify citations and numerals, and fall back to templates.

    No generated future event, inferred identity, automatic offence label or model-only severity ladder.

    Evaluate the sequence, not just message labels

    Linking and evidence correctness

    False merges/splits, source-span match, actor/target accuracy, boundary communication and clock-order abstention. One count over the wrong actor is still a failure.

    Whole-stream pattern quality

    Pattern precision/recall, support-event sets, onset delay, false alerts per case-day, episode duplicates, correction/retraction and missing-context failures.

    Real Android / real limits

    Reducer p50/p95, native/Java memory, joules/event, replay, process death, key unavailability and deletion/expiry. No measured phone claim here.

    Leakage-safe evaluation: split by relationship/person/conversation/source. Keep overlapping windows and duplicate artifacts together. Online replay uses only evidence and reviews available at its cutoff, not later imports or final conversation length.
    Functional tests that should hold regardless of model
    • Repeated payloads do not inflate counts; genuinely distinct identical texts remain distinct.
    • Two people named Alex do not silently merge.
    • Missing outgoing records never prove nonresponse.
    • A coverage gap disables no-contact and comparable-rate assertions.
    • A boundary placed after the sequence cannot support an after-boundary claim.
    • Correcting/deleting an anchor invalidates dependent patterns and summaries.
    • Arrival-order replay gives the same final retrospective result, without rewriting detection history.
    • High-density consensual logistics remains a benign contrast.

    Report separate results for English, Malayalam, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Bengali, Marathi, Hinglish, Romanized Indic and code-mixed/slang. Current Sakshi-specific per-language results are unknown. Synthetic fixtures prove rule behaviour, not real-world safety accuracy.

    Hackathon implementation that demonstrates the core problem
    1. Selected text/export + manual events, original preservation and reviewed time/actor links.
    2. Distinct contact units, bounded timeline and gap ledger.
    3. Four cards: counts; boundary-relative recurrence; reviewed insult-to-threat transition; comparable-window density change.
    4. Template explanations with real source anchors and limitations.
    5. Late import, duplicate, correction, deletion and expiry replay tests.
    6. Only then add a compact classifier or one foreground local LLM.

    Defer: neural graph training, personalized Hawkes, production change-point alarms, generated futures, psychological profiling, cross-app identity automation and violence-risk scores.

Preserve the incident.
Do not promise the original.

WhatsApp does not provide a documented Android route for Sakshi to extract or reopen a recipient's original View Once item. Notification observations and user-imported artifacts remain useful, but must be labeled honestly.

The audio exception changes the answer. Current official WhatsApp Help explicitly says native screen recorders can record audio of View Once messages. That is a possible manual user workflow, not proof Sakshi can capture audio automatically or retrieve the original voice file. Official evidence

B. Current official evidence

These passages were inspected in rendered official Help Center pages on 2 October 2026. Android runtime outcomes remain untested.

Decisive nuance / WA1

Audio is not secure pixels

“It's possible for iOS or Android users to record audio of view once messages using the native screen recorder.”

Do not claim all audio recording is impossible. Do not claim a third-party recorder inherits system capture authority.

Lifecycle / WA1

14 days from sending

“Once you receive a view once photo, video, or voice message, you’ll need to open it within 14 days of it being sent. Otherwise, it’ll expire from the chat.”

A notification time is not a verified source-send time. No trustworthy Sakshi expiry countdown follows from it.

Item sharing / WA1

No original-item share route

“You can’t forward, copy, save, star, or share photos, voice messages, or videos that were sent or received with view once media enabled.”

Adding an Android share receiver cannot compel WhatsApp to publish the attachment.

Backup / WA1

Unopened is a special case

“View once media and voice messages can be restored from backups if the message is unopened at the time of back up.”

The same paragraph says opened items are not included and cannot be restored. Neither rule grants Sakshi extraction or replay.

Photo/video screenshots, recordings and gallery

WhatsApp's Android instructions say photos/videos are not saved to recipient Photos/Gallery, cannot be forwarded/shared/copied, and recipients cannot take screenshots or screen recordings of View Once media. The separate About article acknowledges native audio recording. Read both, not just one. WA2 / WA1

Opening, server retention, receipts and reporting

After viewing, chat shows Opened; once the viewer is exited, the media cannot be viewed again. WhatsApp documents that encrypted media may remain on its servers for a few weeks. Neither statement proves instantaneous forensic erasure or grants recovery permission.

Reported View Once media/voice is provided to WhatsApp. Photo/video reporting has an official in-viewer UI. Specific View Once help conditions Opened receipts on read receipts; general help lists group/play-receipt exceptions. Do not guarantee viewing is invisible or invent post-consumption reporting availability. Report sources

C. Android permissions do not create source access

Notification listener

Observes app-published notifications, not protocol messages. Labels, Person and time fields may be absent or ambiguous. Reposts, summaries and history can inflate counts.

Locked chats hide sender/content. Removal is not consumption/deletion/expiry. No universal View Once extra.

Projection + secure windows

FLAG_SECURE prevents secure pixels in screenshots/non-secure displays. Session consent does not override that restriction.

Exact WhatsApp flag implementation was not inspected. Honor documented source protection rather than assume a client-specific internal mechanism.

Playback audio

Third-party capture needs explicit projection consent, microphone permission, same profile, eligible usage and source policy allowing it.

Most restrictive policy wins. System-only capture can differ from ordinary app capture. Voice notes are not automatically VoIP calls.

Accessibility

Nodes can expose UI labels, not original frames or sound. Screenshot APIs include a secure-window failure. Sensitive nodes have separate protection.

No broad UI scraping, auto-open or fake disability-tool declaration. Not required for MVP.

Share + pickers

Receivers consume what a source app actually shares. SAF selects exposed documents; Photo Picker selects images/video, not arbitrary audio.

A separate native recording is an independent import. A URI without the owner's grant is not access.

Sandbox + storage

UID/process/SELinux isolation protects app-private files. Broad storage access does not expose another app's app-specific data.

No private cache harvesting, provider guessing, backup extraction, session material or replay workflow.

D. A supported preservation flow

No media capture is presumed at notification arrival. The user controls viewing, recording, importing and disclosure.

Statement / context

Save remembered wording, approximate incident time and user-described sender/type with uncertainty. If permitted, import ordinary surrounding messages, profile details or a placeholder screenshot.

No hidden-content OCR, protected-viewer screenshot, or complete-chat promise. Advanced Chat Privacy or its latest-client replacement, Restricted chat, can block export. Restricted chat adds linked-device restrictions; legacy chats do not upgrade automatically.

Native audio artifact

User independently uses normal stock recorder behavior, reviews output and selects a file. No overridden protection, automatic recorder launch or fallback recapture.

Preserve received container, extracted audio and STT separately. Silence/truncation stays explicit. Import does not remove outside gallery/cloud copies.

WhatsApp report

Guide users to the native reporting process. Explain that View Once media/voice and other report data can be provided to WhatsApp.

No automatic submission, groundless reports, retrieved payload or guaranteed enforcement. Voice-specific menu remains phone-untested.

Independent lawful copy

A selected permitted file can be organized as an independently supplied artifact with user-asserted relationship to the incident.

Official Help acknowledges external recapture as a privacy limit, not legal clearance or an Android original-item export. No external-capture workaround mode.

E. Where the feature must stop

No capture fallback

Secure-pixel, source audio, URI or export denial ends that route. Do not switch APIs or target protection failures.

No recovery/replay

No consumed-item recovery, restore cycles, modified clients, private files or sessions. Previously acquired independent artifacts are not recovery.

No invented payload

Metadata cannot reveal hidden speech or pixels. Repeated media labels do not prove harassment, intent, escalation or guilt.

No exploitation, encryption bypass, hidden database, malware or circumvention methods were researched. The full report classifies 16 proposed workflows and their safe alternatives.

F. Four independent permission gates

01 / Android

Actual API authority

Notification grant, capture session and selected URI grant each authorize different data. None grants the original View Once item.

02 / WhatsApp

Provider controls and terms

Respect sharing, secure-content and one-view lifecycle controls. Native-audio possibility is not a provider integration approval.

03 / Distribution

Google Play policies

Necessary permissions, prominent disclosure, affirmative consent and secure expected use. No notification theft, silent transmission or false accessibility-tool claim.

04 / Law and rights

Recording and evidence use

Phone ownership, receipt or a safety purpose is not universal legal permission to record/retain/disclose. Obtain market-specific review; no court-admissibility promise.

User safety takes priority over capture. Neutral alerts should be optional and off by default. Do not pressure users to view distressing items. Viewing/listening may affect receipts. No automatic report, block or deletion.
Vault, backups, recorder files and key loss

Use app-private storage and Keystore-backed authenticated encryption; no plaintext logs or fallback. Background collection needs a tested key-access design, not assumed biometric availability. Exclude evidence and derivatives from unwanted cloud/D2D/migration; allowBackup=false alone is not a universal OEM guarantee.

The stock recorder can place files in shared storage or synced galleries before import. Sakshi encryption does not remove those copies. Explain key-loss and recovery limitations. A hash detects stored-byte changes, not authenticity, authorship or guilt.

G. A defensible hackathon scope

Core

Observation and incident editor

Optional narrow NLS collector; Unknown-first parser; user statement and corrections; permitted share/SAF/picker inputs. Manual mode always works.

Optional demo branch

Tested native-audio import

Use a real harmless stock-phone recording only if demonstrated. Import it as a separate recording, then run local STT on actual audible data.

Core

Provenance and review

Encrypted immutable artifacts, input hashes, separate OCR/STT/model outputs, timeline and reviewed explicit export. No cloud AI or backend needed.

Five records, with no invented original-file hash

Notification observation: minimal exposed fields, distinct clocks, summary/repost state, parser and consent epoch.

Incident statement: original user account, creation time, user-described incident time, uncertainty and revisions.

Imported artifact: actual bytes/MIME/hash, selected import mechanism, acquisition category and user-asserted source relationship.

Derivative: parent hash, OCR/STT/model version/language, extraction boundaries, uncertainty and edits.

Finding: evidence links, observed/user-reported/inferred/pattern/unknown status and human review. No media-content finding without media input.

Phone tests that must precede support claims

  • All three harmless View Once modalities and ordinary-media controls, with actual notification fixtures.
  • Locked chats, foreground/background, preview settings, groups, bursts, listener revocation and reconnect.
  • One stock visual restriction check without overrides; native voice/audio recording and real-file import with output verified.
  • Native video-audio behavior tested separately; do not infer pixels or voice success from it.
  • Normal sharing/export privacy boundaries, voice-report UI, viewer exit/interrupted play and silent recordings.
  • Offline vault, disk/key failures, backup/migration exclusions and user-reviewed export.

None of these phone tests ran here. A long-running 14-day expiry test cannot be certified by a rushed demo or changed clock. Synthetic fixtures remain labeled synthetic. The full report supplies a 17-row verification protocol and 12 acceptance requirements.

H. Claims Sakshi must not make

“Captures every View Once item”

No protected-pixel/original-file route or complete arrival feed was established. Generic media labels are not View Once proof.

“All audio recording is impossible”

Contradicted by official native-recorder audio documentation. Equally wrong: “the native recorder works, so Sakshi can automatically record it.”

“Recovers consumed media”

After-exit recovery, guaranteed replay and backup extraction are not supported. Unopened-at-backup restoration does not grant Sakshi access.

“Permission overrides protection”

Projection, accessibility, all-files access and phone ownership do not create protected-item entitlement.

“Original, authentic, court-admissible”

Recordings, statements and context have distinct provenance. Hashes do not prove sender, source time, guilt or legal admissibility.

“Invisible and guaranteed lawful”

No guarantee viewing/listening is unnoticed, recording is legal everywhere, report action succeeds, or a missing notification means no incident.

Sakshi helps you preserve information exposed in opted-in notifications, your own incident account, and evidence you explicitly import. It cannot retrieve or reopen original View Once items. Supported native audio recordings may be imported as separate artifacts; availability and permissions vary.

The full report lists 28 explicit prohibited claims, including false backup, report readback, provider approval and unseen-content AI claims.

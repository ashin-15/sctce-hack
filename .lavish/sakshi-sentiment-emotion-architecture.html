Three questions, three different answers

Inferred / optional

What emotion is expressed?

Anger, fear or joy in the selected text. Multiple labels can coexist. This does not establish a person's internal state.

Intensity: unavailable in the MVP. A raw label score does not measure how strongly someone feels.

Inferred / reviewable

What conduct may be described?

Narrow toxicity signals plus independent cues and user concern. Quotes, roles, consent and missing context change the interpretation.

A low score cannot block preservation. Calm language can still warrant review.

Observed facts / reviewed events

What happened over time?

Distinct selected events, intervals and reviewed boundaries. Interpretations of repetition remain evidence-linked and correctable.

Partial history stays partial. No prediction of danger, guilt or future violence.

Preservation and review do not depend on emotion

Selected imports retain their received originals before analysis. Passive notification observations follow a separate consented retention policy; they are partial observations, not a universal chat archive.

The user reviews the claim and its sources

Keep outputs distinguishable

  • Observed: selected source content, event intervals, exact distinct-event counts.
  • Inferred: expressed emotion, behaviour suggestions, quoted-text roles.
  • Pattern: interpretation supported by multiple reviewed events, with source IDs and gaps.
  • Unknown: unsupported language, missing context, unavailable model, unestablished identity.

Corrections must propagate

  1. Confirm preservation separately from the interpretation.
  2. Reject or edit the suggested label and its role attribution.
  3. Invalidate affected context, retrieval and pattern versions.
  4. Rebuild counts from distinct reviewed events; keep audit history.
  5. Export only the user's reviewed selection.

Canonical persisted text anchors use Unicode code points. Kotlin UTF-16 indices require an explicit conversion; they cannot be copied into the shared event contract.

What must be shown before Android claims

Not established

Task and language qualification

Case/conversation-safe splits; rare-category recall; quote, negation and consent controls; per-language and code-mix metrics; calibration and abstention on deployment-like data.

Not established

Native parity and device resources

Actual tokenizers and final graph; shape/padding parity; cold and warm latency; peak memory; thermal and battery measurements on physical Android devices.

Required design gate

Evidence and review correctness

Originals preserved, spans mapped, duplicate notifications handled, gaps labelled, rejection changes counts, invalid output remains unknown, unsupported language still preservable.

Required design gate

Offline and privacy behaviour

Airplane mode after provisioning; no cloud fallback, raw-content logging or silent sharing; key-lock, cancellation, backup and deletion paths exercised.

Research deferred beyond the MVP

Emotion intensity requires an annotated rubric, ordinal/regression evaluation and user comprehension testing. On-device training, federated learning, broad multilingual claims and large graph/sequence models are separate research tracks. A hash verifies consistency against a known digest; it does not prove authenticity or legal admissibility.
